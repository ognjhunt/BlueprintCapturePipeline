What Blueprint does (owner direction, 2026-11-12):
- A site films one recurring job once. Blueprint turns the video into a job packet that any robot deployment company can evaluate without visiting, sends it to deployment companies Blueprint has approved, and tells the site who will take the job and on what evidence.
- The site is the user and pays nothing. The deployment company is the customer; how it pays is not decided, and no price is stated anywhere. Each introduction is recorded as a billable event. Nothing is charged and no price is stated until the owner sets one.
- The job packet: the site's own sentence for the job and its constraints; the video with the site's rights recorded; the first look on paper; the simulation of the site with the job's object in it; the success rule; and, where one exists, the result and a clip of Blueprint's early trial of openly available robot software.
- The site's answer is the responses of deployment companies. Until they exist the site sees the first look and the simulation, and is told plainly where the job stands.
- `Blueprint-WebApp` is the site side. `BlueprintCapturePipeline` is the packet factory. `BlueprintCapture` is kept only for its `extractFrames` upload hand-off; its phone apps are not in use.

Required behavior:
- Treat the job packet as the product and the simulation as a means, not the product.
- Label Blueprint's own test "an early trial of openly available robot software". It is supporting evidence; it is not the site's answer and does not predict what a deployment company's own robot would do. A zero is a valid result. A scripted run only checks Blueprint's own simulation.
- Sharing is the service. A site that sends a job agrees once, in the statement it accepts (recording statement `2026-11-12.v4` or later), that Blueprint shares the job with the deployment companies it has approved: the site's description, its video without its sound, the simulation built from it, the success rule, the first look and any early trial. There is no share button, no share setting and no pause. A job under an older statement is not shown to any company until its site accepts the current one. A site's name, street address and contact details are never shared without asking the site first. The site asks for an introduction with one press, at most three per job; Blueprint then gives each side the other's name and contact details by email. Nothing else shares them. No person at Blueprint stands between a site's video and an introduction. When a job is ready it is shown to every approved deployment company, and those that asked for job emails are told. People act only where their own decision is the point: a site sends, confirms, asks for an introduction and can withdraw; a company applies and answers; Blueprint approves a company once. After an introduction both sides are asked, at 14, 45 and 90 days, what happened; the answer is recorded on the job.
- Keep the only hard stops: privacy, rights and money. Flag everything else and keep going.
- Use the words "deployment company", "job packet", "first look", "simulation of your site", "early trial", "responses".

Do not:
- Describe the simulation or the early trial as the site's answer, or say Blueprint picks the robot that did best.
- State a price, a response that has not been given, or a promise of a response.
- Call a deployment company a vendor, integrator or OEM.
- Describe the capture app, outside capturers, payouts, hosted sessions, world-model packages or city launch as current.
- Start new work on what is paused: hardening the automatic test for any kind of job, several objects or unattended scale; standing re-tests of every robot on every open job.
- Invent new system boundaries without checking the shared block first.

Output:
- A grounded summary, recommendation, or plan tied back to the job-packet model above.
