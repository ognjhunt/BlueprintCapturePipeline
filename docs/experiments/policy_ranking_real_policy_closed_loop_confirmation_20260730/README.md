## What changed

Blueprint's existing model-neutral `policy_wam_closed_loop` harness is retained.
A generic DROID Ctrl-World transition adapter now binds the released interfaces:

1. query the same frozen OpenPI DROID policy;
2. preserve its complete native 10x8 or 15x8 joint-velocity/gripper output;
3. apply Ctrl-World's released learned joint-velocity state adapter;
4. condition exactly one Ctrl-World WAM on six history rows plus five future
   Cartesian-pose rows;
5. require five generated frames for each of the three registered camera views;
6. propagate commanded joint, gripper, and Cartesian state;
7. re-query the same policy from generated views only.

The public ten-row rule is preserved prospectively: `pi0_droid` and
`pi0_fast_droid` repeat their final action row to reach Ctrl-World's 15-row
adapter input. `pi05_droid` supplies 15 native rows. Every arm executes the first
eight rows and advances exactly 8/15 seconds per interaction.

Policy-DROID is deliberately excluded from this arm. Its absolute-joint-position
contract is not converted into Ctrl-World's joint-velocity interface.

## Claim boundary

This is not an exact reproduction of the Ctrl-World paper. The historical
OpenPI and policy revisions used by that paper remain unpublished. Current
official Ctrl-World and OpenPI revisions are frozen prospectively, so the arm is
named **Blueprint Ctrl-World current-reference**.

The public Ctrl-World `droid_new_setup_full` examples contain 45 three-view
validation episodes with a success field, but all 45 are marked successful and none identifies the policy
or checkpoint that produced the outcome. They cannot establish a blind
multi-policy physical ordering. The previously unsealed 17-session cohort also
remains diagnostic-only.

The separate public trajectory `droid_subset/val/899` exposes `success: 0` and
was inspected during this availability audit. Its label is excluded from the
prospective input packet; the trajectory is engineering-only and can never be
used as blind confirmation.
